Learning Affordance Posteriors for Robotic Grasping from Human Video Priors

UC Berkeley — Tomizuka MSC Lab

Learning robot-executable grasp affordances from human video priors, using successful simulated grasps to turn human-contact regions into posterior supervision.

My contribution: Led the video-to-3D reconstruction pipeline and affordance-model training.

31.9% → 61.6% average grasp success over human-prior supervision on unseen object instances and categories.

Research overview

The pipeline reconstructs hand–object interactions from egocentric and third-person videos and extracts object-centric contact priors. Successful simulated grasps within these regions provide posterior labels for affordance learning. A learned affordance model then guides a conditional diffusion model to generate grasp poses.
